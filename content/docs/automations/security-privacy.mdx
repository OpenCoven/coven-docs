Automations repeat actions without a human in the loop, so the security review
starts from a hostile assumption: anything that can make a run dispatch, widen
its authority, or falsify its evidence will eventually be tried.

## Authentication and replay

Ratified requirements ([#857](https://github.com/OpenCoven/coven/issues/857)):

- Every mutating command carries authenticated principal context, a stable
  adoption key, and a nonce; replayed commands and replayed approval or nonce
  values are rejected, not re-executed.
- Principal credentials support rotation and revocation. A revoked principal
  fails closed at the next dispatch.
- Command adoption commits before consequential side effects, so a replayed
  request is idempotent rather than a second execution.

The landed control actions predate this contract: they accept an `intentId`
correlation hint but do not yet enforce nonce/adoption semantics. Until the
ratified contract lands, restrict who can reach the daemon (same-user local
IPC) and treat the [approval guardrails](/docs/automations/approvals) as
normative targets.

## Binding failures that must refuse

Every dispatched run pins principal, familiar root/revision, definition
revision, occurrence fence, runtime descriptor, and capability/approval
evidence. Each of these inputs must refuse dispatch when it is unknown, stale,
malformed, revoked, or unavailable. Required refusal vectors:

- forged `familiarId` or principal strings;
- stale or revoked identity revision;
- approval reused after a definition or action change;
- runtime capability mismatch or downgrade;
- a duplicate scheduler or worker presenting an old fence;
- forged client-authored run, approval, or receipt state.

## Confused deputy and capability escalation

A confused deputy is a run that uses its legitimate authority for an
attacker-chosen goal. The automation defenses:

- Authority is bound to the action, not to the prompt: **prompt text, tags,
  runtime names, and creation-time approvals are not authority**
  ([Identity and authority](/docs/automations/identity-authority#what-is-not-authority)).
- The runtime receives only the bounded execution projection it needs — never
  credentials, unrelated identity history, private audit material, or ambient
  authority.
- Capability grants are explicit, per-operation, and pinned; a client payload
  cannot expand them.
- Cross-familiar or cross-project delegation requires its own binding; one
  familiar's authority never silently covers another's work.

## Time-of-check to time-of-use

Authorization, binding commit, and dispatch are separate moments, so TOCTOU is
a first-class test axis: identity revocation during queued, awaiting-approval,
dispatching, and running states; approval expiry races; policy snapshots going
stale between decision and dispatch. The ratified rule is that authorization and
the dispatch-binding commit operate on the same immutable snapshot boundary, and
policy changes never rewrite history — revocation during a run is explicit and
audited.

## Redaction: secrets, prompts, memory, paths

- Logs and errors must never print credentials, raw approval secrets,
  unrestricted prompts, private memories, or unrelated filesystem paths.
- Run logs are bounded and privacy-classified.
- Sensitive identity, prompt, memory, and authority payloads are separated from
  public/operational receipt fields, and encrypted at rest where the deployment
  profile requires it.
- Prefer stable opaque IDs and digests over copying declarations or personal
  data into every row or event.

## Access control for events, history, and receipts

- A changefeed subscriber receives only the fields authorized for its principal
  and client profile.
- Read access to history and receipts is authenticated and profiled; "local
  same-user" is the deployment boundary, not an authorization model.
- Receipts carry a privacy/retention class and a redaction status so consumers
  know what they are allowed to retain.

## Retention, redaction, erasure, tombstones

Retention and erasure must not break the minimum audit evidence:

- Retention bounds apply to logs, events, and receipts without breaking
  recovery or verification evidence.
- Erasure and redaction semantics preserve the minimum security/audit evidence
  while removing personal data.
- Deletion tombstones definitions and preserves required history
  ([Lifecycle](/docs/automations/lifecycle)); there is no command that erases
  run history.

## Exactly-once limitations

Coven does not claim exactly-once external effects. The honest model:

- **No duplicate fenced execution** — one occurrence fence cannot own two
  accepted runs; that is enforced and tested.
- **At-least-once observation** — observers may see evidence more than once;
  consumers deduplicate.
- **External effects are not transactional** — an effect in an external system
  cannot be rolled back by Coven, and an ambiguous one is marked
  `recovery_required` and resolved by a human with evidence
  ([Delivery](/docs/automations/operations/delivery)).

Any claim that an automation executed an external effect exactly once is
misrepresentation unless the external system itself provides the transaction.

## Conservative language

Products and integrations built on automations must not claim: certified
security, legal agency or personhood for a familiar, ownership of effects,
correctness of generated content, or effects Coven could not verify. The
[receipt semantics](/docs/automations/identity-authority#what-an-automationreceipt-proves)
define what is provable; everything else needs hedged, conservative wording.
