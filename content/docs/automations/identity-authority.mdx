A routine carrying `familiarId = "charm"` is not proof that Charm ran, that the
current principal authorized the run, or that the runtime had permission to
perform its effects. Authority is established fresh, at dispatch time, by
explicit binding — never inherited from creation time, prompt text, or naming.

## Familiar root versus display alias

A familiar's display name or alias is not its identity. The Familiar Contract
owns a stable **familiar root** identity plus an **identity revision**; a
definition may name an alias, but the alias must resolve to exactly one stable
root or the dispatch refuses with a typed, durable reason. Ambiguity is not
resolved by best guess.

The exact-revision embodiment binding (familiar-contract#17) pins, for every
run: the familiar root ID, the exact identity revision and declaration digest at
decision time, and the validity/revocation/retirement status at that moment. A
paused, retired, or revoked familiar fails closed.

## Authenticated principal binding

Every dispatched run resolves an authenticated principal — a stable, opaque
principal identity plus a credential/key reference or authorization proof —
and records it in the run's execution binding. Creation-time identity,
credentials, approvals, runtime availability, and authorization are never
silently reused: identity and authority are resolved after the occurrence
claim, immediately before dispatch.

## Capability and risk classification

Ratified risk vocabulary, aligned with the organization-wide agent risk classes:

| Class | Meaning | v1 default policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R0 | Read-only local analysis, no protected mutation | May run unattended only under an explicit narrow grant |
| R1 | Bounded local artifact creation in an approved scope | May run unattended only under an explicit narrow grant |
| R2 | Mutable local state or migrations | Requires a reviewed policy with deterministic fixture/rollback contract |
| R3 | Network, credentials, user data, remote APIs, publication, external effects | Requires per-run or tightly scoped recurring approval and protected-owner policy |
| R4 | Identity, authorization, persistence control, release, deletion, security-critical mutation | Requires per-run or tightly scoped recurring approval and protected-owner policy |

New and imported routines are paused by default. Prompt text cannot self-declare
its risk class or grant capabilities; classification comes from the authority
profile, not from the payload.

## Authority decisions

The Threads authority profile resolves each action to one of:

- **permit** — dispatch proceeds;
- **requires approval** — dispatch waits within a bounded policy or skips/fails
  according to a declared approval-misfire policy;
- **degrade to proposal** — the action is not executable as scheduled; it
  becomes a proposal for a human to act on;
- **reject** — the dispatch fails closed with a typed, durable reason.

Unknown, stale, malformed, incompatible, or unavailable policy inputs refuse
dispatch. There is no string-only fallback and no unbound launch when an
authority adapter is missing.

## Approval lifecycle

```text
not_required
required -> requested -> approved | rejected | expired | revoked
approved -> consumed | revoked | expired
```

Ratified requirements: an approval records the exact definition revision,
occurrence, action digest, capabilities, familiar revision, and intended
runtime; any relevant change after approval invalidates it; approvals are
operation-specific, bounded, expiring, non-transferable, and consumed or
otherwise replay-safe; an approval for one occurrence cannot authorize another
occurrence, a changed definition, or expanded capabilities; and no client can
directly write `approved` into run state. See
[Approvals](/docs/automations/approvals) for operator behavior.

## Runtime capability binding

Before launch, the run pins the exact runtime descriptor, version, and
capabilities, selected to satisfy the action's declared requirements, together
with the selection rationale. A runtime that cannot meet the declared
requirements refuses dispatch. Capability downgrade — a runtime arriving with
fewer or different capabilities than pinned — is a refusal, not a degradation.
See [Runtimes](/docs/automations/runtimes).

## What an AutomationReceipt proves

Every terminal run commits a versioned `AutomationReceipt` (ratified in
[#857](https://github.com/OpenCoven/coven/issues/857)) that can be verified
independently of Coven internals: all definition/occurrence/run/attempt
correlation, the exact familiar and principal binding, authorization/approval/
capability decision digests, the runtime descriptor and session correlation,
exercised capabilities and declared side-effect class, start/finish/terminal
disposition, normalized result/artifact/delivery digests, retry/cancellation/
recovery history, producer identity and integrity fields, and the privacy/
retention class.

**A receipt proves what Coven observed and authorized.** It does not prove:

- model intent or why the model acted;
- legal agency, personhood, or ownership of effects;
- correctness of generated content;
- completion of effects Coven could not verify (for example, an external
  system's state after a network call).

Where an external effect cannot be observed, the receipt records the ambiguity
instead of papering over it. See
[Receipts](/docs/automations/operations/receipts) for verification guidance and
[Security and privacy](/docs/automations/security-privacy) for the threat model.

## What is not authority

Each of these is a common misrepresentation, and none of them authorizes a run:

- **Prompt text** — prose inside a definition cannot grant capabilities, declare
  a risk class, or widen scope.
- **Tags** — labels are metadata, not authorization evidence.
- **Runtime names** — naming a runtime does not make that runtime permitted for
  the action.
- **Creation-time approval** — an approval captured when the definition was
  created does not authorize later runs; authority is resolved fresh per run.
- **A familiar display name** — `familiarId` routes context; it does not
  authenticate a principal or bind an identity revision.
