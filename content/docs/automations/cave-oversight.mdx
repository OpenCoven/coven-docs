Cave is OpenCoven's first-party application surface. For automations it is the
oversight plane: propose, create, approve, inspect, and recover — while Coven
remains the only lifecycle authority.

## What Cave does

- **Proposes and creates** definitions through the Coven control plane.
  Definitions land paused, whether written by hand or imported.
- **Approves** operation-specific actions through the authority profile
  ([Approvals](/docs/automations/approvals)).
- **Inspects** occurrences, runs, leases, and health through read-only
  projections.
- **Surfaces recovery controls** — pause, cancel, reconcile — as client flows
  over the daemon's supported actions, never as local state surgery.

Cave already reads the Coven control plane instead of direct Codex ownership
([coven-cave#4990](https://github.com/OpenCoven/coven-cave/pull/4990)).

## What Cave must not do

- Author scheduler policy or edit run state.
- Synthesize `successful`, `running`, `authorized`, or `healthy` state that the
  daemon did not report.
- Keep a parallel automation ledger or history that could disagree with Coven's.
- Treat its local approval UI as authority: approvals are daemon-consumed
  evidence with adoption keys, not client-side booleans.

## Truthful degraded states

When Coven reports stale, ambiguous, or degraded state — an expired lease, an
unverifiable receipt, a stale familiar identity — Cave renders that truth. A
polished UI that hides incomplete certification behind a healthy badge is worse
than an honest warning; the program's non-goals forbid it
([#854](https://github.com/OpenCoven/coven/issues/854)).

## Compatibility facade retirement

Cave previously ran automation definitions from harness-owned homes (Codex's
`~/.codex/automations/`) and spawned runtimes directly. The ownership migration
moved that onto the Coven control plane. A compatibility facade remains for
older definitions and surfaces; its deprecation and retirement are tracked in
[coven-cave#5217](https://github.com/OpenCoven/coven-cave/issues/5217):

1. New definitions, imported or authored, go through Coven and stay paused until
   reviewed.
2. The facade keeps reading legacy sources without deleting them during the
   migration window.
3. Retirement removes the facade reading and writing paths once oversight is
   complete — it never deletes user source files.

For the migration mechanics and the review checklist, see
[Migration from Codex](/docs/automations/migration-codex).
